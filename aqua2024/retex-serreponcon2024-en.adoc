== Context

A quiet landing after a takeoff from Saint-Vincent-les-Forts where it ultimately doesn't hold.
I'm the only one in the group to go ("Always up for a fly, we always learn!").
Very calm conditions and a light breeze over the Serre Ponçon lake.

== Description of the incident

I decide to work on the box landing (called PTU in French) because I always have a hard time estimating the altitude at which to make the final branch.

.Planned PTU vs realized PTU
image::serreponcon-ptu.png[{full-width}, "Planned PTU (blue) vs realized PTU (yellow)"]

I lose altitude upwind of the terrain when I see a paraglider approaching in front of me (facing the breeze, in S-turns), but lower.
He takes an infinite amount of time to come down (maybe he was letting me pass?) so that I find myself at his height.

I decided to launch my initial box plan by extending my branches to pass behind him (I think I am high enough).
I ended up too short and landed in the lake (5m from the edge, but without a foothold).
Fortunately, a swimmer pulled me towards the edge, and the breeze gently carried my glider out.

No physical damage (I am not talking about my Syride alas…) but big frustration of having to spend hours drying everything and folding the reserve the next morning (thanks, friends).
And then big blow to morale imagining how I am going to get rotten by my friends (I am Pelican31 now ;-)

== My analysis

How the hell could I have been so bad?
The pilot in front has absolutely nothing to do with it, his actions and intentions were clear and healthy.
I just rushed.

I think I was a victim of this famous "tunnel effect" so often described in incidents.
I had decided to land in a certain way, and in a certain place, and I stuck to it at all costs, while I had plenty of options (turn in front of him to land on the next beach, very healthy, or cut to aim for the edge of the lake).

A few other remarks:

- Perhaps a little ambitious, the box landing, on such a small landing (but after all, I aim to validate the French "confirmed" pilot license, I have to assume).
- I had not thought about any plan B or, in any case, analyzed all the options for landing, while having even thought about it, would perhaps have allowed me to implement them in an emergency.
- I had not anticipated to what extent I would dive once I was at the edge of the beach (I finally found myself on the lee side of the beach).

== What I learned

- That it is good to analyze all possible landings during your quiet loss of altitude.
- That a planned landing is not immutable and that safety must take precedence over priorities or politeness.
- That in good weather a paraglider and a reserve dry quickly, but that lines and harnesses take longer.
- That solidarity between paragliders plays a full role in these great moments of solitude.
- That friends are not so mocking after all, because they saw that I was pissed off.
